How to Dry Herbs at Home (Without Losing Flavour)

Drying herbs is easy; drying them so they still taste of something is the part people get wrong. The rule is simple: dry fast enough to stop mould, slow enough to keep the oils.

Air drying

Bundle stems, tie, hang upside down in a dark, dry, airy room. 1-2 weeks. Best for rosemary, thyme, oregano, sage and lavender.

Oven drying

Lowest setting (50-60C / 120-140F), door slightly open, 2-4 hours. Check every 30 minutes - ovens overshoot and scorch the oils.

Dehydrator

40-45C / 105-115F for 2-4 hours. The most consistent method for soft-leaved herbs like basil, mint and parsley.

How to tell they are dry

A leaf should crumble, not bend. If it bends, it still holds moisture and will go mouldy in the jar.

Storing

Whole leaves keep flavour longer than crushed. Airtight jar, dark cupboard, label with the date. Most herbs hold good flavour for a year.
