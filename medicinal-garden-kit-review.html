Medicinal Garden Kit Review: Is It Worth It?

This is not a paid review - it is the honest version, including the parts the sales page does not mention.

What you get

Seed for a set of medicinal herbs plus a planting and use guide. The common set: chamomile, echinacea, lavender, calendula, lemon balm, marshmallow, feverfew, yarrow, purple coneflower.

The good

  • These are mostly perennials - they come back, so the cost drops after year one
  • Chamomile, calendula and echinacea are genuinely hard to find as cheap local seed
  • The guide covers drying and storage, not just planting

The caveats

  • Germination rate is the thing to check first - sow half the packet and see
  • Climate matters: some species will not overwinter in cold zones
  • If you only want culinary herbs, ordinary seed is cheaper

Who should skip it

Anyone with no outdoor space and no windowsill - these are not houseplants. And anyone who wants a harvest this month: most of these take a season.

Common questions

Is the Medicinal Garden Kit worth the money?

If you want chamomile, calendula and echinacea and have a bed or large pots, yes - mostly because the plants are perennials and return each year. If you only want culinary herbs, buy ordinary seed instead.

What seeds come in a medicinal garden kit?

Typically chamomile, echinacea, lavender, calendula, lemon balm, marshmallow, feverfew, yarrow and purple coneflower.

How long until the herbs are ready to harvest?

Most take a full season. Chamomile and calendula flower in the first year; slower perennials like echinacea are better in year two.
