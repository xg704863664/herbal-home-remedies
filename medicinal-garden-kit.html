Medicinal Garden Kit: What You Actually Get

If you are searching for a medicinal garden kit, the useful question is not whether it is good - it is whether the herbs inside will actually grow where you live, and whether you will use them.

What is usually in a medicinal garden kit

Most kits combine seed for a set of easy medicinal herbs with a planting and use guide. The common list: chamomile, echinacea, lavender, calendula, lemon balm, marshmallow, feverfew, yarrow and purple coneflower.

How much space it needs

A single 1m x 2m bed, or roughly eight to ten medium pots, is enough for a starter kit. The herbs in these kits are mostly hardy perennials - they come back, which is why the cost per season drops after year one.

Who it is not for

If you want herbs for cooking only, buy culinary seed - it is cheaper. Medicinal kits make sense if you specifically want chamomile, calendula, echinacea and the like, which are harder to find as cheap seed locally.

What to check before buying

  • Seed quantity and the sow-by date
  • Whether the guide covers drying and storage, not just planting
  • Whether the species suit your climate zone
