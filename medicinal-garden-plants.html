Medicinal Garden Plants: Nine Worth Growing

These nine are the ones that earn their space: easy to grow, useful, and mostly perennial so they come back each year.

1. Chamomile

Sleep and digestion. Sow direct, flowers in the first year. Self-seeds freely.

2. Calendula

Skin and minor wounds, usually as an oil or salve. Flowers all summer if you keep picking.

3. Echinacea

Traditionally for colds. Slow - better in year two. Needs 45cm spacing.

4. Lemon balm

Anxiety and sleep. Spreads aggressively - sink it in a pot.

5. Thyme

Coughs and sore throats. Wants full sun and poor, dry soil.

6. Sage

Sore throats, traditionally as a gargle. A shrub - give it 60cm.

7. Peppermint

Digestion and blocked noses. Another aggressive spreader - always in a pot.

8. Feverfew

Traditionally for headaches. Very easy from seed; it will find its own place.

9. Marshmallow

Dry coughs and irritated throats. Tall (1.5m+) and needs a permanent spot.

How much space for all nine

Roughly a 2m x 3m bed, or about twelve medium pots for the smaller ones.

Common questions

What are the best medicinal plants to grow?

Chamomile, calendula, echinacea, lemon balm, thyme, sage, peppermint, feverfew and marshmallow - all easy to grow and useful.

Which medicinal herbs spread and need a pot?

Lemon balm, peppermint and feverfew all spread aggressively. Sink them in pots up to the rim if you want to keep them in place.

How much space do nine medicinal herbs need?

About a 2m x 3m bed, or roughly twelve medium pots for the smaller species.
